All quotes come from public-domain works or traditional proverbs; attributions were checked against their sources.

  1. 2

    Fate shuffles the cards; we play

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · GermanDas Schicksal mischt die Karten, und wir spielen. (영역: “life is a game of cards, when the cards are shuffled and dealt by fate.”)

  2. 4

    A man shows his character in trifles

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · EnglishA man shows his character just in the way in which he deals with trifles,—for then he is off his guard.

  3. 8

    Wealth is sea-water: drink, and thirst

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · EnglishRiches, one may say, are like sea-water; the more you drink the thirstier you become; and the same is true of fame.

  4. 16

    Reading: thinking with another's head

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · GermanLesen heißt mit einem fremden Kopfe, statt des eigenen, denken. (영역: “Reading is thinking with some one else's head instead of one's own.”)

  5. 32

    Style is the physiognomy of the mind

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · GermanDer Stil ist die Physiognomie des Geistes. (영역: “Style is the physiognomy of the mind, and a safer index to character than the face.”)

  6. 64

    We take our own limits for the world's

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · EnglishEvery man takes the limits of his own field of vision for the limits of the world.

  7. 128

    A hypothesis feeds only on what fits

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · EnglishWhen an hypothesis has once come to birth in the mind, or gained a footing there, it leads a life so far comparable with the life of an organism, as that it assimilates matter from the outer world only when it is like in kind with it and beneficial.

  8. 256

    Self-control alone frees us from force

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · EnglishNothing will protect us from external compulsion so much as the control of ourselves.

  9. 512

    Fame must be won, honour only kept

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · GermanRuhm muß daher erst erworben werden: die Ehre hingegen braucht bloß nicht verloren zu gehen. (영역: “Fame is something which must be won; honor, only something which must not be lost.”)

  10. 1024

    We mix because we cannot bear solitude

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · GermanWas nun andrerseits die Menschen gesellig macht, ist ihre Unfähigkeit, die Einsamkeit und in dieser sich selbst zu ertragen.

  11. 2048👑 Crown quote

    Life swings between pain and boredom

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · EnglishThus its life swings like a pendulum backwards and forwards between pain and ennui.

    Willing comes of want; want is pain

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · EnglishBut the basis of all willing is need, deficiency, and thus pain.

    Each perfection borders on a flaw

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · GermanJede menschliche Vollkommenheit ist einem Fehler verwandt, in welchen überzugehen sie droht; jedoch auch umgekehrt, jeder Fehler, einer Vollkommenheit.

    In youth we await the curtain's rise

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · EnglishIn early youth, as we contemplate our coming life, we are like children in a theatre before the curtain is raised, sitting there in high spirits and eagerly waiting for the play to begin.

    — Arthur Schopenhauer

    Original · GermanDas Talent gleicht dem Schützen, der ein Ziel trifft, welches die Uebrigen nicht erreichen können; das Genie dem, der eines trifft, bis zu welchem sie nicht ein Mal zu sehn vermögen.
